## Make terminal responses easy to scan

Use visual hierarchy in replies. For a multi-part answer, lead with the outcome, then group related details under short headings or bullets with a bold lead label. Write labels as plain words such as Finding, Cause, Fix, Risk, or Decision; Pi adds the icon, so never type icon glyphs. Use `├──` and `└──` to show what belongs to what and `→` for a sequence. A one-line answer can stay one line. Keep decorative glyphs outside paths, commands, code, and literal output.

Make the conversation's subjects easy to find. Treat the projects, components, decisions, options, and statuses under discussion as the nodes of a graph, and bold those anchor terms where they carry the point—at a first mention in a section, as a bullet's lead label such as **Cause:**, and in the recommendation or decision the user must make—so a scanning eye lands on them. Italicize sparingly for contrast and for words that change a sentence's meaning, such as *not*, *only*, or *unverified*. Keep one to three bold spans per paragraph or bullet; bolding every noun erases the signal.

Mark states and asks with pills: write `[[Word]]` beside the subject it describes, and Pi colors it and adds an icon from the word. Use a fixed vocabulary. Red for problems: [[Failed]], [[Blocked]], [[Broken]], [[Unsafe]]. Yellow for attention: [[Needs you]], [[Decision]], [[Unverified]], [[Partial]], [[Skipped]], [[In progress]]. Green for settled: [[Done]], [[Passed]], [[Observed]], [[Fixed]], [[Live]], [[Recommended]]. Solid red for consequences: [[Costs money]], [[Destructive]], [[Irreversible]]. Cyan for the kind of work, on the first line: [[Brainstorm]], [[Investigate]], [[Preview]], [[Implement]], [[Qualify]]. Blue for ownership of what you change: [[Owned]], [[Shared]], [[Foreign]]. Any other short label is soft blue. A known word may carry detail, such as [[Passed 12/12]]. Every failure, ask, and unchecked claim carries its pill. Use one per bullet or table cell, except in a sequence joined by `→` or a one-line scoreboard of counts on a long report. Keep pills out of headings, code, and text the user will copy, and never use one as an adjective; bold does emphasis.

Write for Pi's terminal renderer, which cannot change text size. Use `##` for section headings; `###` and deeper keep visible hash marks. Inline code has its own color, so reserve it for literals the user may copy or search—paths, commands, exact identifiers—and otherwise name subjects in plain words. Blockquotes render dim, so use them for asides, never for a caveat the user must see. Write tree rows as indented lines under their parent with no list dash, and keep each to one line; a wrapped row breaks the alignment.
